# Type and relations of four-generated numerical semigroups

Lean formalization accompanying the [manuscript](../article.pdf).

## Main result

For every numerical semigroup minimally generated by four positive integers,

`t(S) + 1 ≤ η(S) ≤ 2 t(S) + 3`.

- `FourGeneratedSemigroups.type_and_relations` states both bounds for the minimum cardinality of a presentation.
- `FourGeneratedSemigroups.minimal_presentation_type_and_relations` states both bounds for every inclusion-minimal presentation. Equality of all such cardinalities is proved.
- `FourGeneratedSemigroups.presentation_type_lower_bound` applies to every presentation.

A numerical semigroup is an actual additive submonoid of the natural numbers with finite complement. The hypotheses require four positive generators that generate this submonoid and are minimal. Type counts the actual integer pseudo-Frobenius numbers. Presentations generate the actual additive congruence of equal-weight factorizations. No ordering, ideal-dimension equality, or combinatorial bound is assumed.

## Proof and algebraic interpretations

The development constructs finite Apéry sets, factorization congruences and critical relations, the necessary generator reordering, finite lower sets and their boundaries, and actual polynomial defining ideals and Artinian reductions. Standard-monomial and socle bases prove the type comparisons. Planar injections establish both three-dimensional boundary/corner inequalities. Polynomial congruence invariants establish the relation-loss bound. Positive weighted-homogeneous generator comparison proves minimal-presentation cardinal invariance.

The polynomial defining ideal, semigroup-ring quotient, and socle results are valid over every field. The final numerical-semigroup inequality has no field hypothesis. The proof is structural and does not rely on finite enumeration of semigroups or sampled graphs.

## Scope

The complete main theorem, including both inequalities and all minimal presentations, is covered. Corollary 1.2's separate identifications of actual Tor/Betti numbers, the sharpness examples, and the supplementary computational tables are not formalized. They are not premises of the main theorem.
